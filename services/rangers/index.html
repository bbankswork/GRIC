Protecting Our Communityand Its Resources

The Gila River Indian Community's Rangers have a history of helping protect the Community's lands, natural resources, and cultural resources. Their work has included patrolling off-road areas and assisting law enforcement personnel in locations beyond established roadways.

The Community's large geographic area makes awareness of its land and resources an important part of public safety. Rangers have historically contributed to that work through patrol and coordination with other public safety personnel.

The Land

Knowledge of the Land.Commitment to the Community.

The Gila River Indian Community spans more than 583 square miles across seven districts. Serving this landscape requires an understanding of the Community's geography, its resources, and the people who live here.

Rangers' historical role reflects the importance of protecting places that may be far from main roads while supporting the wider public safety mission.
